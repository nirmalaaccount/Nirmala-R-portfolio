01 — Overview

I started with a simple question.

“ Snapchat has grown far beyond Camera + Chat. But do users
actually know what else they can do — and does the product
make those experiences easy to discover when they matter? ”

Role

Product Designer

Research

13 + 3

Focus

Connect · Create · Explore

02 — Research

Research before redesign.

I didn't want to start by deciding what Snapchat should look like. I wanted to understand what people actually do, what they know about the product, and where discovery breaks down.

01 · Survey13 responses to understand usage patterns, awareness and preferences.
02 · Interviews3 conversations to hear how people describe their real Snapchat behavior.
03 · Product + competitor auditReviewed core Snapchat experiences and competing discovery patterns.
Primary research

The strongest pattern was simple: people came with a reason.

In my sample, Camera and Chat were the most familiar behaviors. That made me question my first assumption that the problem was simply “too many features.”

13 responsesDirectional survey evidence — not representative of Snapchat's full user base.
3 interviewsQualitative conversations used to understand the “why” behind the behavior.
03 — Research Findings

What I heard changed the question.

01

Users mainly came for Snap + Chat.

The familiar path was strong. People didn't open Snapchat thinking about which feature to explore next.

02

Awareness and usage are not the same thing.

Knowing that a feature exists does not automatically mean knowing when it is useful.

03

Discovery often happens incidentally.

Secondary experiences can be encountered while navigating rather than intentionally sought out.

04

Context matters.

Relevance, navigation and privacy can shape whether someone continues exploring a feature.

05

Instagram appeared stronger for some discovery/content tasks in my sample.

This is a sample-level observation, not a claim about all users or the market as a whole.

04 — User Journey

The user journey I was trying to improve.

The problem wasn't one broken screen. It was the gap between a user's initial intention and the broader set of experiences Snapchat could offer.

01 · ARRIVE

I have a reason.

Open Snapchat to chat, capture or check something familiar.

02 · COMPLETE

I do the thing.

The core task is usually fast and familiar.

03 · DISCOVER

What else is useful?

Secondary features need to become relevant, not merely visible.

04 · EXPLORE

Give me a reason.

Content, places and social experiences should have clear context.

05 · RETURN

Bring value back.

Discovery can reconnect to friends, creation and sharing.

06 — Key Insights

Five insights became my design constraints.

Insight 01

Habit is powerful.

Redesigning Snapchat cannot come at the cost of the Camera + Chat behaviors people already understand.

Insight 02

Feature count isn't the same as feature value.

The product needs to communicate why an experience is useful, not simply that it exists.

Insight 03

Discovery needs context.

A feature is easier to explore when it appears in a moment where it makes sense.

Insight 04

Privacy changes behavior.

Location experiences need control and clarity, especially when social discovery is involved.

Insight 05

Bring exploration back to people.

Discovery becomes more meaningful when it can reconnect with friends, creation and sharing.

07 — Problem Definition

The problem I chose to solve.

Snapchat has grown into a broad ecosystem, but users' behavior can remain concentrated around familiar Camera and Chat interactions.

The problem I chose to solve was how to make the rest of the ecosystem easier to discover and understand without disrupting the core experience.

08 — Opportunity

The opportunity wasn't “add more.”

Make Snapchat's existing ecosystem easier to discover at the right moment + context.

For people

Less searching.

Help users understand what an experience is for and where to enter it.

For the product

More connected journeys.

Let discovery, creation and social interaction reinforce one another.

For the business

More meaningful engagement.

Create more opportunities for repeat use, content interaction and monetizable surfaces — to be validated, not assumed.

14 — Final Design

Final design: stories, not a screen dump.

Each story starts with a real user situation and ends in an experience.

I grouped the final work by the moments I wanted to improve. This makes the UI easier to evaluate as a product journey rather than as isolated screens.

STORY 01

Create a new group / discovery.

A social entry point that helps people move from seeing friends to starting a shared conversation.

Design intentMake group creation feel like a natural continuation of the social graph.
User valueLess friction between discovering people and acting on that relationship.
STORY 02

Discover a new feature.

Help users understand what Snapchat can do without forcing them through a feature catalog.

Design intentIntroduce secondary experiences through context instead of adding more permanent navigation.
User valueUsers can learn something new without having to know the feature name first.
STORY 03

Keep the magic fast.

Camera remains the heart of Snapchat. The redesign focuses on making lens discovery easier while preserving the capture flow.

Design intentPreserve the camera-first entry, improve lens browsing and keep creation actions close to the thumb.
Product valueProtect a familiar behavior while creating a clearer path to more of Snapchat's creative system.
STORY 04

Give Explore a clearer job.

Move from a collection of content surfaces toward a more intentional discovery experience.

Design intentMake content, categories and discovery pathways easier to understand at a glance.
User valueExplore becomes something users can choose intentionally, not something they stumble into.
STORY 05

Plan together.

Connect discovery with people so Snapchat can support a shared plan rather than a solo browsing session.

Design intentUse social context to make places and activities more useful.
Business opportunityMore meaningful place and activity discovery can create additional engagement surfaces to validate.
STORY 06

Turn location into an experience.

I didn't want to remove Snap Map. I wanted to give it a clearer reason to exist: discover something worth experiencing, then bring that moment back into Snapchat.

FlowDiscover a place → Experience it → Capture the moment → Share it.
GuardrailLocation discovery should remain privacy-conscious and user-controlled.
STORY 07

Let discovery lead somewhere.

Creator experiences give content discovery a destination and a reason to continue interacting.

Design intentConnect content, creator identity and social action.
Product valueMake creator discovery feel connected to the broader Snapchat ecosystem.
STORY 08

Make AI useful, not just present.

AI should help with a specific user job rather than becoming another destination that users have to learn.

Design intentGive assistance a clear role in discovery and decision-making.
ValidationMeasure whether task-based assistance actually reduces time or confusion.
STORY 09

Bring identity back into the system.

The profile experience closes the loop between what a person creates, discovers and shares.

Design intentMake personal activity and identity feel connected to the wider ecosystem.
System valueSupport continuity between social, content and personal experiences.
15 — Design Decisions

The decisions behind the screens.

01 · Camera

Why keep Camera prominent?

Because the research pointed back to familiar Camera + Chat behavior. A redesign should build on a habit before trying to replace it.

02 · Map

Why not remove Map?

Low or selective usage does not prove a feature has no value. I treated awareness, usefulness and privacy as the design problems to solve.

03 · Explore

Why create clearer discovery?

Because the ecosystem is broader than the habitual path. The goal is to make exploration easier to choose, not harder to avoid.

04 · AI

Why task-based assistance?

A generic assistant creates another blank destination. A specific job gives the user a reason to engage and gives the product something measurable to test.

05 · Sharing

Why connect discovery back to people?

Because social context is one of Snapchat's strongest assets. Exploration becomes more meaningful when it can return to friends and shared moments.

06 · Scope

Why redesign the ecosystem, not every detail?

I focused on the journeys most connected to the research question. The goal was a coherent product direction, not a cosmetic replacement of every screen.

16 — What I Would Validate Next

What I would validate next.

The final designs are a concept. I would not claim that they increase engagement or revenue without testing them. The next step is to put the riskiest assumptions in front of users and measure behavior.

Test 01

Can users discover the right experience?

Give participants tasks such as “find a new lens,” “discover something nearby,” or “share a place with a friend.” Measure success, time and confidence.

Test 02

Does Map feel useful and safe?

Test whether users understand the value of place discovery and whether privacy controls feel clear enough to proceed.

Test 03

Does the new IA reduce confusion?

Compare the proposed mental model with the existing navigation using findability and first-click tasks.

Test 04

Does discovery lead to meaningful action?

Measure whether users move from content or places into actions such as sharing, saving, creating or connecting.

17 — Success Metrics

Success metrics.

I would measure both sides of the experience: whether users can do what they came to do, and whether the product creates healthier opportunities for repeat engagement and monetization.

Feature discoveryCan users find experiences they didn't already know?
Task successCan users complete key journeys without getting lost?
Meaningful actionsShares, saves, creations, conversations and place actions.
Repeat useDo users return to more than one meaningful product area?
Content interactionQuality engagement with Spotlight, Stories and creators.
Map engagementUseful place discovery, not simply more map opens.
RetentionReturning users after exposure to the new experience.
MonetizationDownstream impact on ad, creator and premium opportunities.
Guardrail metrics matter too: privacy concerns, notification fatigue, content quality, negative feedback and any increase in passive consumption without meaningful value.
18 — Limitations

Limitations.

Small primary sample

13 survey responses and 3 interviews provide directional evidence, not a representative picture of Snapchat's entire user base.

No internal product data

I did not have access to Snapchat analytics, retention cohorts, monetization data or experimentation infrastructure.

No controlled usability test yet

The proposed metrics are validation plans. I am not presenting fabricated improvements as measured outcomes.

Competitive observations change

Competitor products evolve continuously, so the audit represents the experiences reviewed during this project.

Concept scope

The work explores product direction and interaction design rather than production engineering or backend feasibility.

Market claims stay cautious

Sample-level preference observations are not treated as broad market conclusions.

19 — Learnings

What I learned.

01

Don't let the redesign become the assumption.

I started with the idea that Snapchat might have become too complicated. Research made me narrow the problem instead.

02

Feature awareness is a product problem.

Building a feature is only part of the job. Users need to understand when that feature is useful.

03

Existing habits are product assets.

Camera and Chat aren't obstacles to redesign. They are anchors the new experience should respect.

04

Business value starts with user value.

More engagement is not the goal by itself. The stronger question is whether users have a meaningful reason to return.

20 — Next Steps

From concept to validation.

If this moved from concept to product, I would start by testing the highest-risk assumptions before scaling the experience.

01 · Test

Run usability sessions across the key journeys with a larger, more diverse participant set.

02 · Experiment

A/B test the highest-confidence information architecture and discovery changes against the existing experience.

03 · Measure

Connect user behavior to retention, quality engagement and business outcomes over time.

Final thought

I didn’t redesign Snapchat to make it look different.I redesigned the journey to make it make more sense.

Connect with people. Create a moment. Explore something worth sharing. That became the simple idea behind the redesign.
