01
THE PROBLEM

Visibility isn’t the same as value.

I started with a broad assumption: Snapchat’s features might be difficult to discover. But early research suggested a more nuanced problem. Some people use Camera and Chat by choice. Others miss friends’ Stories or find Spotlight less relevant. And some avoid location sharing intentionally.

Initial assumption

“Make every feature easier to find.”

This could lead to an unnecessary navigation overhaul.

Reframed opportunity

“Help people find the content they care about.”

Improve discovery without interrupting established habits.

What it means

Don’t mistake preference for friction.

Strong Camera and Chat habits—and relatively positive navigation ratings—argue against changing Snapchat’s primary navigation without better evidence.

Research boundary: Survey questions had different response counts. The findings identify possible opportunities, not proven usability failures.

Why explore this with limited research?

The survey and interview were sufficient to generate a hypothesis, not to prove a widespread problem. The contrast was useful: many respondents understood the app, while Spotlight use and relevance appeared less compelling. I therefore treated Explore as a narrow, reversible prototype to investigate—not as a recommendation to ship.

Evidence that would change my mind: If additional participants find Stories easily and understand Spotlight already, I would stop pursuing navigation changes and investigate content relevance separately.

05
ALTERNATIVES & PRIORITIZATION

Three directions. One focused bet.

The goal was not to produce the biggest redesign. It was to select the direction most closely tied to the evidence, with the lowest disruption to familiar behavior.

A · Not selected

Rebuild the navigation

Restructure Snapchat’s main tabs to expose more features.

Trade-off: Large behavioral disruption, with limited evidence that navigation is broadly broken.

HIGH DISRUPTION
B · Selected

Give Explore a clearer job

Separate friends’ Stories from broader content discovery and make each destination understandable.

Why first: Best match to observed awareness and relevance signals, without changing Camera or Chat.

TEST FIRST ↗
C · Defer

Expand social experiences

Explore group discovery, planning, local places and map-led sharing.

Trade-off: More scope, privacy considerations and weaker connection to the central research question.

FUTURE EXPLORATION
This is a design prioritization decision, not a proven product outcome. The alternatives are conceptual directions, not documented usability-tested iterations.
Existing destinations first

Clarify before adding.

The concept should make existing Stories and Spotlight destinations more understandable. The storyboard includes other discovery categories, but those are exploratory; this case study does not establish a need for five new tabs or a separate permanent destination.

Smallest useful experiment

Test the hierarchy, not a platform redesign.

Begin with content grouping, entry-point labels and prominence in a clickable prototype. Keep Camera and Chat unchanged. Only expand the information architecture if testing shows an unmet need.

06
PRIMARY FIGMA CONCEPT

Explore becomes a destination with a purpose.

The primary storyboard groups discovery into more intentional areas for Stories, Spotlight, creators, sounds and other content. The hypothesis: clearer categories could help people understand where to look, while leaving Camera and Chat untouched.

Design iteration · proposed next cycle, not completed work

01 / Baseline

Observe where participants go today to find a friend’s Story and a relevant Spotlight video. Capture errors, hesitation and existing expectations.

02 / Compare

Test a minimal Explore hierarchy against the current experience. Ask participants to explain category labels in their own words.

03 / Refine

Keep only the groupings that improve findability without slowing Camera or Chat. Remove unclear or unnecessary categories.

Why no before-and-after screens? Only the original exploratory Figma storyboards are documented. Showing fictional iterations as completed design work would overstate the process.

08
VALIDATION PLAN & REFLECTION

The next step is evidence, not another screen.

I would test a clickable Explore prototype against the current Snapchat experience, using the same tasks with participants. The goal is to see whether discovery improves without making core actions slower.

Decision criterion — compare behavior, not aesthetics

If participants find Stories more successfully and understand the proposed labels without additional Camera/Chat friction, refine and retest. If not, remove the extra hierarchy and investigate relevance or content quality as a separate problem. No numerical threshold is claimed because no baseline test has been conducted.

What I learned

Don’t redesign what users already value.

Research challenged my initial assumption. The stronger design question became how to improve discovery without treating intentional non-use as a defect.

What I would do next

Test, learn, then refine.

Recruit a broader participant group, evaluate comprehension and task performance, then iterate the Explore hierarchy based on observed friction.

No usability test results, launch metrics or business impact are claimed here. This case study documents an independent concept and proposed next steps.
Decision gates

What would make me change or abandon this direction?

Stop

Participants already find friends’ Stories reliably, or the proposed grouping adds confusion without measurable task improvement.

Revise

People discover the right content type but misunderstand category labels, or the change adds steps to reach Camera or Chat.

Continue testing

Participants find target Stories more consistently with no meaningful loss in Camera/Chat speed. Then test with a larger, more diverse sample.

User value

Meaningful discovery, not scrolling.

Success would mean finding a specific friend’s Story or a relevant piece of content with less effort—not simply increasing views or time spent in Spotlight.

Product value · hypothetical

Measure quality and trade-offs.

Potential measures: successful Story opens, intentional Spotlight follows or shares, repeat relevant-content visits, and retention. Guardrails: hidden stories, unwanted views, and delays returning to Camera or Chat.

These are proposed metrics and test criteria. No results, uplift or business impact have been measured.
