00 · Product Design Case Study

From discovery to a more confident purchase.

A self-initiated Meesho product exploration. I looked at how users discover products, what makes them hesitate, and how the experience could help them move from browsing to a more confident purchase.

Role · Product DesignerScope · UX + UI + Product ThinkingPlatform · MobileType · Self-initiated
“I did not want to redesign all of Meesho. I wanted to understand where users need more confidence, then improve the moments that matter.”
01 · Overview

It started with a simple question.

How might Meesho help shoppers move from “Is this worth exploring?” to “I feel confident buying this”?

My role
Product Designer · research, UX, UI and concept strategy
My approach
Product audit · competitor review · qualitative conversations · design exploration
Final output
Enhanced discovery · occasion-led shopping · redesigned PDP
Scope: This is a self-initiated concept. I did not have Meesho internal analytics or access to customers at scale, so the final screens are design hypotheses, not proven business results.
02 · Business Context

Why I chose this problem.

Meesho already gives users a very large selection and strong price value. I was more interested in what happens when a user is interested in a product but is still unsure whether it is the right choice.

USER SIDE

The user needs confidence.

Before buying, a shopper may ask: Is this product right for me? Can I trust the seller? When will it arrive? What did other customers receive?

PRODUCT SIDE

The experience needs to support that decision.

Meesho already has many of these signals. The opportunity is to make them easier to notice, understand and use at the right moment.

03 · Market Landscape

I first looked at how other commerce products solve similar moments.

I reviewed Myntra, AJIO, Amazon, Flipkart and selected fast-delivery patterns. I looked at discovery, delivery visibility, reviews, promotions, styling and product confidence.

01

Delivery can be a shopping signal.

When delivery timing is important, users may want to know it before they invest time in a product.

02

Discovery is more than search.

Collections, occasions, recommendations and inspiration can help users who are browsing without one exact product in mind.

03

Proof helps the final decision.

Reviews, customer photos, seller information and product details help users judge what they are about to buy.

My takeaway: I used competitor patterns to understand possibilities, not to copy features into Meesho.
04 · Product Audit

Before designing, I checked what Meesho already had.

This was important because I did not want to create a solution for a problem the current product had already solved.

DISCOVERY

There are already many signals.

Categories, filters, price, ratings, reviews, offers and product cards are already part of the browsing experience.

PDP

The confidence information already exists.

The PDP already has product images, size, seller, product details, reviews, customer photos, delivery, returns and purchase actions.

MY OBSERVATION

The problem looked more like hierarchy.

The shopper has to move through the experience and connect different pieces of information themselves.

“My assumption changed. I stopped asking, ‘What information is missing?’ and started asking, ‘What information should be easier to see at each decision point?’”
05 · Research Approach

I used two layers of research.

01 · SECONDARY

Product + competitor research

I audited the current Meesho journey and reviewed other commerce experiences to understand where discovery, delivery and purchase confidence show up.

02 · QUALITATIVE

Short shopper conversations

I spoke with two shoppers about how price, quality, delivery time and confidence affect their purchase choices.

Research limitation: The qualitative sample is small and directional. I used it to understand behaviours and shape hypotheses, not to represent all Meesho users.
06 · Qualitative Research

Two shopping stories changed the direction.

SHOPPER 01

Low price was not enough for every purchase.

The shopper was comfortable using Meesho for lower-risk products. For office or wedding wear, they were more cautious because of quality, wrong-product and delivery concerns.

What I took from it: when the purchase feels important, users want more proof before committing.

SHOPPER 02

Urgency changed the choice.

The shopper needed white jeans quickly. Meesho was cheaper, but slower delivery made another marketplace feel like the better option for that situation.

What I took from it: when there is a deadline, delivery can become part of the product decision.

The common theme was not “Meesho has no trust.” It was that price, quality proof and delivery timing work together when a shopper decides what to buy.

07 · Evidence

Then I went back to the actual Meesho screens.

I wanted to separate what I observed from what I was assuming. These screens became the starting point for the design.

Current PLP · DiscoveryCategories, filters, price, ratings, reviews, offers and product cards are already part of browsing.
Current PDP · Product decisionProduct, size, seller, details, reviews, customer photos, delivery and purchase actions are already available.
“This changed the direction of the project. Meesho already had the ingredients. I needed to make the shopping journey easier to understand and easier to use.”
08 · Hypotheses

The questions behind the redesign.

H1 · DISCOVERY

Earlier useful signals may help shortlisting.

If delivery, value and trust cues are easier to see while browsing, users may be able to narrow their choices more easily.

H2 · OCCASION

Context may make discovery easier.

If an occasion banner opens a focused shopping page, users may discover relevant products without starting with an exact search.

H3 · PDP

A clearer decision layer may improve confidence.

If product proof, size, styling, delivery, seller information and reviews are easier to scan together, users may feel more confident evaluating the product.

Important: These are hypotheses I would validate. They are not claims that the redesign will automatically increase conversion.
09 · Problem Definition

The problem I chose to solve.

Shoppers may hesitate when the information they need to evaluate a product is spread across the journey, especially when quality confidence or delivery timing matters.

How might we?

Help users discover relevant products, shortlist them with useful signals, and make the final purchase decision with less uncertainty?

10 · Opportunity

Three moments. Three jobs.

01
DISCOVER

Give users a reason to explore.

Help people start from an occasion, category, collection or recommendation instead of only an exact search.

02
SHORTLIST

Show useful signals while browsing.

Make delivery, value and trust cues easier to notice when users decide which products are worth opening.

03
DECIDE

Bring the important proof together.

Make the PDP easier to scan around product, size, styling, delivery, seller and customer proof.

12 · User Flow

Two entry points, one decision journey.

OCCASION ENTRY

Occasion need → Occasion page → Curated products → PDP → Purchase

PRODUCT ENTRY

Product search → Enhanced discovery / PLP → Shortlist → PDP → Purchase

I did not want to create a completely new shopping model. Both paths eventually bring the user to the same purchase-confidence moment: the PDP.

14 · Final Design

Three connected design changes.

These are the final screens I designed. Together they tell one story: discover → explore in context → decide with confidence.

01 · Enhanced Home / Product DiscoveryI created more ways to discover through Explore Categories, festival content, personalised picks, Budget Buys, Deal of the Day, Express Delivery, Flash Deals, Community Styling, curated collections and Popular Picks. Product cards also make delivery, ratings and trust cues easier to scan.
02 · Occasion-led ShoppingI turned the Navratri promotion into a complete shopping destination with a countdown, quick categories, festive deals, fashion, jewellery, puja and decor, gifts and sweets. The structure can be reused for other relevant occasions.
03 · PDP / Purchase ConfidenceI redesigned the PDP around the final decision: product proof, size, Styling Studio, Style This Look, transparent look pricing, delivery, seller information, similar products and customer reviews, with Add to Cart and Buy Now always available.

Why these three screens?

The first screen helps users discover. The occasion page helps them discover with a specific context. The PDP helps them decide whether the product is right to buy. I gave each screen a clear job instead of trying to solve everything on one screen.

15 · Design Decisions · Discovery

How I changed the first screen.

“I wanted discovery to feel useful, not just busy. The user should have different ways to enter shopping depending on what they already know.”

01 · MORE ENTRY POINTS

Support different shopping intentions.

Categories, personalised picks, Budget Buys, deals and collections help users who are browsing without one exact product in mind.

02 · DELIVERY

Show timing earlier.

Eligible products show information such as Get it by Tomorrow, so delivery can be considered while shortlisting.

03 · TRUST

Make confidence easy to scan.

Ratings, customer content, trusted-seller cues and the Trust Guarantee give quick context before the user opens a product.

16 · Design Decisions · Occasion

Why turn a festival banner into a full shopping page?

01 · BANNER → JOURNEY

Make the promotion useful.

Instead of taking users to a generic listing, the Navratri banner opens a page built around the occasion.

02 · CURATION

Group products around a need.

Fashion, jewellery, puja, decor, gifts and sweets let users explore even when they don't know the exact product name.

03 · REUSABLE

Use the structure again.

The same experience can be adapted for Diwali, Raksha Bandhan, wedding season and other relevant moments.

04 · ROLE

Keep it focused on discovery.

The occasion page gets users into the right context. The product page still handles the final purchase decision.

17 · Design Decisions · PDP

The PDP is where the final decision happens.

This was not only a visual refresh. I changed the hierarchy and connected the information around the questions a shopper is likely to have before buying.

01 · PRODUCT + PRICE

Understand the product quickly.

The top brings the product image, rating, product name, price, discount and key seller/trust information together.

02 · SIZE

Answer the size question early.

Size selection is placed close to the main product information so the shopper doesn't have to hunt for it.

03 · STYLING STUDIO

Show how the product can be worn.

The Styling Studio gives different looks around the product and answers the next question: “How would I wear this?”

04 · STYLE THIS LOOK

Make inspiration shoppable.

Complementary products are shown with individual prices and a total. The shopper chooses what to add rather than having everything added automatically.

05 · DELIVERY

Make the expected date easy to notice.

Delivery information is brought closer to the buying decision because timing can change what a shopper chooses.

06 · SELLER + CUSTOMER PROOF

Keep marketplace confidence visible.

Seller information, ratings, customer photos and reviews help the shopper judge both the product and the buying experience.

The main PDP idea: I wanted the shopper to answer four simple questions without searching through the page: What is it? Can I trust it? When will I get it? What did other buyers experience?

18 · Validation

How I would validate the solution.

Because this is a self-initiated concept and I don't have Meesho behavioural data, I would not claim that the redesign already improved conversion. I would test the experience first.

DISCOVERY TEST

Can users shortlist more easily?

Give users the same shopping task on the current and redesigned discovery experience. Observe time to shortlist and which signals they use.

OCCASION TEST

Can users discover without exact search?

Give users an occasion-based task and see whether they can find relevant products from the Navratri-style experience.

PDP TEST

Can users answer purchase questions faster?

Ask users to find size, delivery, seller, reviews and product details, then compare the current and redesigned PDP.

What I would look for: task success, time to find information, confidence after the task, add-to-cart behaviour and any negative signals such as returns or cancellations.

20 · Learnings

What I learned from the project.

01 · AUDIT FIRST

Don't assume the product needs more features.

I started by thinking Meesho needed more trust. The audit showed me that many trust signals already existed.

02 · SCREEN PURPOSE

Every screen should have a job.

The discovery screen is for exploring, the occasion page is for contextual discovery, and the PDP is for the final product decision.

03 · DELIVERY

Delivery can influence discovery.

When a shopper has a deadline, delivery is not only a checkout detail. It can change which products they choose to consider.

04 · HONEST VALIDATION

A finished UI is not proof.

The designs are hypotheses. Real validation would need broader usability testing, behavioural data and business guardrails.
