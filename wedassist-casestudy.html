07 · Design Decision 01
Make the service easy to understand

Start with clear choices: packs or individual services.

The final experience puts the two service packs first, then gives users an alternative path to select individual services. This makes the commercial model understandable before users enter the detailed journey.

Supreme · Prime · Individual services
08 · Design Decision 02
Keep selection focused

Turn a service requirement into a simple category-selection step.

For pack-based assistance, users can choose the wedding-service categories they need. The drawer keeps the list scannable and makes selected items immediately visible before submission.

09 · Design Decision 03
Design the states, not just the flow

Verification needed its own complete interaction model.

The experience covers contact capture, OTP entry, invalid OTP feedback, resend behavior and mobile-number correction. Each state keeps the user inside the same task instead of creating a dead end.

Contact details → OTP → validation → resend / update number
10 · Design Decision 04
Make the transaction transparent

Collect only what the consultant needs, then show exactly what the user will pay.

Before the service moves forward, the experience asks for wedding date and city. The payment summary then breaks down the selected service, GST and total amount before the user proceeds.

Wedding details → payment summary → payment outcome → expert follow-up

13 · Reflection

What this project changed in how I design.

01

Requirements are only the starting point.

A PRD defines what needs to be solved; design still has to determine how the experience behaves across real interactions and states.

02

A flow is more than its happy path.

Validation, selection limits, payment review and failure states are part of the product, not edge-case decoration.

03

Human + digital need continuity.

WedAssist taught me to design the transition between a digital transaction and a human service.

04

Product design happens with a team.

The strongest decisions came from translating requirements, reviews and constraints into a coherent customer experience.

The strongest outcome wasn't a single screen. It was turning a complex assisted service into a journey that felt understandable from the first click to the handoff.
