01
What I noticed

The challenge wasn't
tracking tasks.
It was connecting the work.

Project progress lives in task boards, design files, documents, discussions, and status meetings. Teams spend time reconnecting these pieces to understand the whole project and decide what to do next.

The problem

Fragmented tools and handoffs

Milestones, task ownership, approvals, decisions, and project documents are managed in different places, making alignment and follow-up harder.

The opportunity

One place to understand the project

Bring portfolio health, team priorities, project context, decision history, approved documents, and trustworthy AI summaries into one connected workspace.

02
Research exploration

Five perspectives.
Four recurring challenges.

I interviewed five people: a product manager, a product designer, a frontend developer, a UX researcher and a QA engineer. I asked how they track work, handle updates and make decisions, and what went wrong last time. I turned what I heard into four themes. The questions below are my summary of what each person needed, not word-for-word quotes. Five interviews is a small sample, so I treat this as design direction, not a validated study.

Product manager / Interview 01
“What's actually at risk—and who owns the next step?”

Needs a reliable project-level picture connected to real blockers, milestone impact, and recent decisions.

01 / PROJECT VISIBILITY

Updates were fragmented

Teams relied on multiple sources to work out the current state of a project.

02 / DECISION AWARENESS

Changes were easy to miss

Scope changes and approvals could be hard to trace across conversations.

03 / KNOWLEDGE ACCESS

Current documents were unclear

Finding the correct version and owner took additional effort.

04 / COORDINATION

Follow-ups were repetitive

Repeated status checking and dependency follow-ups created friction.

The incident

A stalled vendor listing revamp

The product manager described a vendor listing revamp where development tasks weren’t updated in Jira and a developer was waiting on design clarification. To find the real blocker, the PM had to contact several people, and preparing the leadership status report took about two hours.

The pattern

The same story, four more times

A frontend developer waited almost a day for missing form-validation rules. A QA engineer lost about four hours because Jira said “Ready for QA” before the build was deployed. A designer couldn’t tell whether engineering was using the latest approved design. A researcher never heard that a recommendation was postponed.

03
Competitor research

Five competitors.
One design opportunity.

I reviewed Jira, Asana, monday.com, ClickUp, and Notion across project tracking, dashboards, collaboration, knowledge management, and AI. This helped me identify patterns worth carrying forward and frame an opportunity for a more connected, role-aware experience.

01 / JIRA + ASANA

Work management

Strong task, ownership, and progress tracking. I explored ways to connect task-level signals with a concise cross-project overview.

02 / MONDAY.COM + CLICKUP

Flexible dashboards

Extensive team views, automation, and collaboration. I focused the concept on helping each role see the decisions and changes most relevant to them.

03 / NOTION

Connected knowledge

Flexible documentation and project context. This reinforced the value of surfacing approved versions and source-linked decisions inside the delivery flow.

04
What I designed

Six connected spaces.
One product story.

The solution is a shared delivery workspace, not a blocker dashboard. Each space answers a distinct question while keeping project context connected.

01 / PORTFOLIO

Home dashboard

Where are we across projects? Surface portfolio health, upcoming launches, and projects needing attention.

02 / PROJECT

Project workspace

What's happening here? Bring together ownership, milestones, progress, launch readiness, and activity.

03 / PERSONAL

My Work

What should I do next? Give every role a focused view of assigned tasks and follow-ups.

04 / ALIGNMENT

Changes & Decisions

What changed and who agreed? Keep a traceable history of approvals, scope changes, and acknowledgements.

05 / KNOWLEDGE

Documents

Which information is current? Organize relevant PRDs, research, designs, and QA plans by version and approval state.

06 / INTELLIGENCE

AI Project Brief

How do I communicate progress? Turn authorized project evidence into a reviewable, source-backed briefing.

09
What I learned

Good visibility isn't
more information.
It's better judgment.

The biggest design learning was that an effective project workspace must connect information across roles and tools. Portfolio health, personal priorities, decisions, approved knowledge, and AI summaries need to feel like one coherent experience.

The design lesson

Design around users' questions, not around disconnected tool categories. Make progress, ownership, decisions, and verified information easy to navigate.

What I'd validate next

The prototype hasn’t been tested with users yet. I would test the core journeys: locating project health, finding a current document, acknowledging a decision, completing an assigned action, and reviewing an AI brief. I would measure task success and confidence, and check whether a PM can find the owner and record a next step without making a wrong status change.
