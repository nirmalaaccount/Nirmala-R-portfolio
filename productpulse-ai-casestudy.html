01
What I noticed

The issue wasn't
missing updates.
It was missing context.

A PM sees “On track.” A designer says “Blocked.” A decision is buried in chat. Everyone might be reporting accurately—but the project still feels uncertain.

The problem

Too many disconnected signals

Status information can sit across tasks, messages, documents, design handoffs, and meetings. Reconstructing the full story takes manual follow-up.

The opportunity

One place to understand the decision

Instead of another task tracker, explore a decision-oriented layer that shows the source, owner, recency, and impact behind important updates.

Research framing: this independent concept is informed by workplace observations and exploratory simulated role scenarios. They are treated as design hypotheses, not validated interview findings.

03
What I reviewed

Good tools.
Fragmented answers.

I looked at common patterns across task trackers, knowledge bases, and team communication to define where ProductPulse AI could add value.

01 / WORK TRACKING

Jira / Linear patterns

Strong for individual work and ownership. I explored how workstream issues could inform overall project health without conflating the two.

02 / KNOWLEDGE

Notion / Confluence patterns

Useful for specifications and documentation. I explored how approved versions and relevant changes could be easier to surface.

03 / CONVERSATION

Slack / Teams patterns

Fast for collaboration. I explored how important decisions could remain discoverable after the conversation moves on.

This is a review of familiar tool patterns, not a measured competitive usability study or a claim that these products lack the features discussed.
04
How I defined the solution

From signal
to next action.

I prioritized one high-value PM journey: understand a blocker, decide whether it affects delivery, then record what happens next.

01 — Notice the contradiction

Surface a review warning alongside the last verified overall health. The user shouldn't need to open every team's board to discover the issue.

06
Why I made this decision

What if two people
disagree on status?

I rejected a simple “latest update wins” approach. The PM owns overall project health; a designer can still report a blocked design workstream. Both signals deserve visibility.

Explore the design trade-off

Don't hide the conflict.
Design a review state.

The simpler pattern risks overwriting a useful signal. Separating scopes adds a review step, but creates a clearer responsibility model.

08
What I learned

Good visibility isn't
more information.
It's better judgment.

This project pushed me to design beyond attractive dashboards. The harder question was how to preserve nuance: a green status, a team blocker, a decision still pending, and an AI summary that might be missing context.

The design lesson

Make status scope, ownership, supporting evidence, and approval explicit. Trust needs deliberate interaction design.

What I'd validate next

Observe PMs investigating a blocker. Measure whether they can find the owner, assess impact, and record a next action without making an incorrect status change.
