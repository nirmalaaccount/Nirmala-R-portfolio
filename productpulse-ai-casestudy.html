01 — The challenge

Everyone has an update.
Few have the full picture.

A hypothesis grounded in workplace observations and exploratory scenarios—not a quantified research finding.

A green project status can hide a red team-level blocker.

Product teams work across tickets, design files, documents, chats, and spreadsheets. The challenge is not a lack of information—it is knowing which information is current, approved, and actionable.

01 / VISIBILITY

Scattered signals

Updates live in different tools and need manual consolidation.

02 / ALIGNMENT

Missed decisions

Scope changes may not reach everyone they affect.

03 / TRUST

Unclear ownership

A status label alone does not explain evidence, accountability, or risk.

02 — Design approach

Start with the decision.
Then design the interface.

I framed the concept around one core question: how can a PM understand a project's real state without repeatedly chasing teams?

01

Frame the opportunity

Workplace observations suggested coordination friction. Simulated role scenarios helped explore possible needs; they were not represented as completed user interviews.

02

Prioritize the workflow

Focus on project health, blockers, decision changes, and source-backed briefings rather than recreating a full task-management suite.

03

Design for confidence

Separate team progress from overall health, surface owners and verification, and require human review for AI-generated summaries.

03 — The solution

From signal
to next action.

A focused flow for investigating a blocker—not just another dashboard.

01 / NOTICE

See the risk

Spot a team blocker alongside the last verified overall project status.

02 / UNDERSTAND

Find the context

Open the affected work, owner, source, and milestone dependency.

03 / DECIDE

Assess the impact

Clarify whether the issue threatens the delivery timeline.

04 / ACT

Resolve or escalate

Assign an action and record the rationale before re-verifying health.

04 — A key design decision

Two updates.
Both can be true.

Instead of letting the latest edit overwrite the previous one, ProductPulse treats project health and team-level progress as different kinds of information.

⚠ REVIEW NEEDED

The PM should review the design blocker and confirm its impact before changing overall project health.

Don't choose a winner. Make the conflict visible.

The designer can report a blocker without overwriting the PM's overall assessment. Both records remain visible, with ownership and review state.

Trade-off: an additional review step, in exchange for a more trustworthy status history.

This is a proposed design pattern. Multi-user conflict handling has not yet been verified in the prototype.

05 — Product principles

Useful AI needs
visible boundaries.

SOURCE

Show the evidence

Every briefing claim should link to an authorized underlying record.

UNCERTAINTY

Flag what's unknown

Stale, missing, or contradictory records should not become confident summaries.

CONTROL

Keep people in charge

Users review and approve a briefing before it becomes a shared report.

06 — Reflection

What I'd validate next.

The prototype establishes a design direction, but it does not prove faster coordination or better decision quality. The next step is to test whether PMs can locate a blocker owner, understand its impact, and record a next action without confusion.

I would measure task completion, errors, time to locate authoritative information, and perceived confidence—then revise the interaction based on observed results.

Current boundary: no fabricated interviews, usability results, or impact metrics are claimed. Demo data and illustrative screens are conceptual.
